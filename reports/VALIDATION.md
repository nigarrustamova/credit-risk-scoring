Validator: independent review (MRM, SR 11-7 / ECB TRIM-style) · Date: 11 Nov 2026 · Scope: code, artifacts and claims as of `artifacts/` and `reports/` dated 11 Nov 2026 18:49.

**Method.** I read all modules. I reproduced the hold-out split bit-for-bit in `/tmp/val`: the split seed 7 gives identical `y` and `amount`, and the committed `m2.txt` reproduces `score_m2` exactly. I re-ran the analyses on that split: bootstraps, DeLong, proxy and fairness checks, DTI decomposition, alternative benchmarks, and scorer/API probes. `train.py` was not re-run and no artifacts were overwritten. ## 1. Summary verdict

**Rating: model fit for a portfolio demonstration, but not as presented. The decision layer and the business and regulatory claims need rework before 20 Nov.**

| Area | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Discrimination of M2 | **Sound.** Hold-out AUC 0.794 (95% CI 0.788–0.800). The AUC gain over B1 is highly significant: ΔAUC +0.041, CI +0.037 to +0.045, DeLong z = 19.3. |
| Target / temporal leakage | **No material leakage found.** History tables contain no post-application events. Calibrator, cut-offs, binning and early stopping use development data only. The final tree count (582 = ⌊1.1 × mean(486, 762, 428, 476, 495)⌋) comes from dev CV only. |
| Calibration | **Adequate.** The reported ECE is at the noise floor, so it cannot support a precision claim. The isotonic tails produce PDs of 99.99% and 0.01%. |
| Size of the benefit | **Overstated.** The −12.7% fewer defaulters claim is statistically real (CI −14.8% to −10.4%). Most of the gap, however, comes from capping the scorecard at 15 variables. Against a 50-variable WoE scorecard the benefit falls to −9.0% (CI −10.9% to −6.5%). |
| CBA / DTI decision layer | **Not fit for purpose.** The "existing payments" estimate puts 4.7% of applicants above 100% DTI, and the "applied exactly" claim fails. A missing annuity bypasses the 70% rule. Counter-offers are not re-scored: 45% of them would be declined. |
| Business / AZN illustration | **Misleading.** The "realised loss" uses an early-delinquency flag, the full loan amount as exposure, and a corporate F-IRB LGD. The result is then labelled "lower expected credit loss per year". |
| Explanations / fairness | **Not customer-ready.** Reasons include "Defaults among the applicant's contacts", building characteristics, hour of application, age, and a mislabelled phone reason. Gender is predictable from the inputs with AUC 0.905. The approval ratio for age <25 vs 50+ is 0.67. |
| Engineering | Mostly clean, and the 10 fast tests pass. There are gaps in edge-case handling, input validation and tests of the serving path. |

## 3. Statistical significance (computed on `artifacts/holdout_scores.parquet`, n = 61,502, 4,965 defaults)

Bootstrap: 1,000 resamples of the hold-out (seed 2026), with cut-offs recomputed in each resample. DeLong uses the paired test. These CIs cover **hold-out sampling uncertainty only**, not training variance, the out-of-time effect or population transfer.

| Metric | Point | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| AUC B1 | 0.7529 | 0.7459 – 0.7595 |
| AUC M2 | 0.7942 | 0.7875 – 0.8001 |
| ΔAUC (M2 − B1) | +0.0413 | +0.0374 – +0.0453 (DeLong SE 0.0021, z = 19.3, p ≈ 1e-82) |
| Bad rate at 80% approval, B1 → M2 | 4.87% → 4.25% | B1 4.68–5.07%; M2 4.07–4.44% |
| **Relative bad-rate change at 80%** (claim −12.7%) | −12.7% | **−14.8% to −10.4%** |
| Relative change in "realised loss" at 80% (claim −15.9%) | −15.9% | −18.4% to −13.7% |
| Loss-rate gap at 80% (B1 − M2), percentage points | 0.31 | 0.26 – 0.36 |
| Extra approvals at B1's bad rate (claim +5.4 pp) | +5.4 pp | +4.6 to +6.2 pp |
| Bad-rate change at 70% / 90% approval | −18.2% / −7.9% | −20.9 to −15.3% / −9.5 to −6.3% |
| ECE, calibrated M2 | 0.0019 | 0.0017 – 0.0046 (noise floor for a perfect model: 0.0012 – 0.0038) |
| **Against a 50-variable WoE scorecard** (300 resamples) | ΔAUC +0.026; bad-rate change at 80%: −9.0% | ΔAUC +0.022 – +0.029; bad-rate change −10.9% to −6.5% |

Tie check: B1 has 61,298 unique scores and M2's raw score has 61,502, so both models approve exactly 79.999% at the 80% policy and the comparison is like-for-like. The calibrated PD has only 126 unique values; ranking on it approves 80.16%. The claims correctly rank on the raw score, which has the same order.

**Conclusion.** M2's advantage over *this* B1 is statistically unambiguous. The *magnitude* quoted is specific to a 15-variable benchmark, a Home Credit early-delinquency target and random-split evaluation. It should be quoted as a range (about 9–13% fewer bad loans at 80% approval) with those qualifiers.

## 4. What I confirmed works (for balance)

- Hold-out isolation: the stratified 20% split is reproducible. The isotonic calibrator is fitted on dev OOF only (`train.py:176`). Cut-offs come from dev OOF quantiles (`train.py:208`). B1 binning is fitted inside each training fold. Early stopping uses dev folds only. The final M2 has 582 trees from dev-CV best iterations. No TARGET- or test-derived features: history aggregates are per applicant and contain no target encoding.
- The decision ordering in `decide()` is correct for finite inputs: DTI > 70% → DECLINE; otherwise PD > decline cut-off → DECLINE; otherwise CONDITIONAL if in the review band or flagged. A CONDITIONAL never overrides a DECLINE.
- Calibration by grade on the hold-out is good: predicted and observed are within 0.2 pp in every grade. Calibration by gender is acceptable.
- The scorer reproduces `PD_AT_REQUEST` exactly when given the original application (max |Δ| = 0).

## 5. Questions the audience may ask, and the honest answers

1. **"Is 0.794 AUC what we'd get on our customers?"** No one knows until it is measured on your data. About 0.02 AUC of it comes from Home Credit's third-party scores: without them the same pipeline gets 0.772. A local bureau score could play that role. Expect lower again out-of-time.
2. **"How much better than our scorecard?"** Against a compact 15-variable scorecard: +0.041 AUC and about 13% fewer bad loans at 80% approval. Against a richer 50-variable scorecard built on the same data: +0.026 AUC and about 9% (CI 6.5–11%). Your real uplift depends on what you run today, and the pilot exists to measure it.
3. **"Is the 308k AZN saving real?"** No. It is an illustration: a loss-rate gap on Kaggle data, scaled to a volume you type in, with an assumed LGD and an early-delinquency target. Treat it as a direction, not a number.
4. **"What is 'default' here?"** Home Credit's flag for payment difficulties early in the loan. It is not 90+ DPD and not IFRS 9 / Basel default. PDs and grades would need re-definition and re-calibration on your default definition.
5. **"Does the system enforce the CBA 70% rule?"** The rule engine does, for the inputs it is given. In this demo the existing-debt input is a crude estimate from bureau balances: about 5% of applicants show over 100% DTI, which is not credible. A missing payment value currently bypasses the check. In production the rule must use reported instalments from the credit registry. Cite the exact CBA regulation; the 70% figure in Decision 39/3 as reported concerns guarantors.
6. **"Can you show these reasons to a customer?"** Not all of them. Contacts' defaults, building features, application hour, age and family status should not appear as adverse-action reasons. Several should be removed from the model.
7. **"You removed gender — is it fair?"** Removing gender does not remove it: the other inputs predict gender with AUC 0.90. The approval ratio for under-25s is 0.67 of over-50s. The model is reasonably calibrated within gender, but the fairness review is still to be done with compliance.
8. **"Was the hold-out really untouched?"** Mechanically yes: calibration, cut-offs and the tree count all come from development data, and I reproduced it. Whether design choices were made after seeing hold-out numbers cannot be audited without a development log.
9. **"Is it stable?"** Only across random splits of the same 2018 population. There are no application dates, so no out-of-time test is possible. PSI here is near zero by construction.
10. **"Why not just use the counter-offer?"** Today the counter-offer only checks the debt ratio. When re-scored, about 45% of offered amounts would still be declined by the model. It needs to re-score each offer.
